### Qi et al. 2026
[T2VEval: Benchmark dataset and objective evaluation method for
T2V-generated videos](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S014193822500215X)

- Design choice:
    - The creators of this project wanted to create a T2V assesment model that isn't just based on one score, but evaluates on different metrics to give a more accurate assessment of the generated video. Previous models typically only used a single score to measure the effectiveness of the T2V model. This can ultimately end up obscuring the strengths and weaknesses of the model's performance on certain tasks. By breaking up the initial training of the evaluation model into different dimensions, the final assessment score will more accurately reflect the quality of the video generated by the T2V model. 
    - They chose to evaluate four different dimensions: overall impression, **text-video consistency**, **realness**, and **technical quality**. 

- Implementation note:
    - The authors of this paper chose to evaluate overall impressions by having 73 evaluators judge the quality of their test videos. 
    - Realness, text-video consistency, and technical quality were all evaluated using individual networks. 
        - Technical quality was evaluated using the Swin-3d network.
        - Realness was evaluated by using the ConvLexNet-3D network.
        - Text-video alignment was evaluated using the BLIP network.
    - After each branch has been evaluated, they are sent into a fusion unit. Then they are sent to an LLM to predict the assessment score for the video. 

- Evaluation results:
    - The model proposed in this paper overall outperformed other T2V video assessment models. 
    - When determining the effectiveness of this model, removing any one of the branches led to a decrease in the model's accuracy in predicting an accuracy score. Technical quality overall had the least effect on the overall model's accuracy, meaning that as we determine how to test and evaluate our model we should focus on video-consistency and realness first. 
    - Due to the "divide and conquer" approach (having the separate models for each dimension) the model was able to more accurately evaluate the quality of the T2V videos when compared with other assessment models.

- My Notes:
    - This paper provided a very interesting insight into how to evaluate the quality of AI generated videos. 
    - The "divide and conquer" approach works well with our plans to utilize agents for different parts T2V development. If we chose to implement this structure, or something similar, we could possibly utilize different agents to evaluate each dimension for accuracy before combining the results together into one final score.
